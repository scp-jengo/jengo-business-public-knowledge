# Best Practices: AI Agents in Journalism Support

Standards for AI agents supporting journalism work. The function of journalism is to produce accurate, verifiable information for the public. Every practice here is in service of that function.

---

## Sourcing Standards

**Named sources over anonymous sources.** Anonymous sources are usable for directing investigation, not for establishing claims. A claim from an anonymous source is Tier 3 (niet-achterlijk-protocol) until independently verified. Do not present anonymous-source claims as established fact.

**Primary sources over secondary sources.** When a document exists, cite the document. When a recording exists, work from the recording. Secondary sources introduce interpretation errors. Use them to identify primary sources, not as the evidence itself.

**Independence requirement.** A source who benefits from a story being published is not independent corroboration of the story. Track the interest structure of every source.

---

## Verification Hierarchy

For any claim intended for publication:

1. Primary source evidence (document, recording, physical evidence) — publishable with attribution
2. Independent corroboration from two unrelated sources — publishable with attribution
3. Single source, not independently corroborated — not publishable as fact; may be publishable as allegation with explicit labeling
4. Anonymous source only — not publishable as fact; usable as a lead for further investigation

AI-generated content about events that could not have been observed should be treated as Tier 3 by default.

---

## Attribution Requirements

Every factual claim in a published piece should have a documented source in the production record, even if the source is not named in the publication. The production record must be sufficient to reconstruct how every claim was established.

Distinguish clearly between:
- "X said Y" (attribution to a source)
- "Y is true" (claim of fact)
- "It appears that Y" (inference from evidence)
- "X alleges Y" (unverified claim from a source)

These are not stylistic variants. They carry different epistemic commitments.

---

## Off-the-Record Information

Information provided off the record cannot be published, attributed, or used to identify the source, but can be used to:
- Direct investigation toward verifiable evidence
- Understand context that helps interpret publishable information
- Assess whether a claim is worth pursuing

The boundary must be explicit before the conversation begins, not applied retroactively. If the boundary is unclear, treat the information as off-record until confirmed otherwise.

---

## Separation of Fact and Analysis

Factual claims and analytical conclusions are different things and must be labeled differently. An analysis that is presented as fact is misleading even if the analysis is correct.

Format: establish the facts first, explicitly, with sources. Then label the analytical section clearly. Readers are entitled to evaluate the facts and reach their own analytical conclusions.

---

## AI-Specific Practices

AI agents supporting journalism work should:
- Never generate quotes attributed to real people
- Flag all AI-generated content in the production record
- Not fill gaps in evidence with inference presented as fact
- Apply the niet-achterlijk-protocol to their own outputs, not only to external sources
