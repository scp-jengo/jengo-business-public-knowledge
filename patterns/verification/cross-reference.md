# Cross-Reference Methodology

How to cross-reference claims across sources. The key distinction: corroborating sources (independently obtained the same evidence) vs. laundering sources (citing each other, creating the appearance of independent confirmation).

---

## Core Principle

Multiple sources reporting the same claim is not the same as multiple sources independently verifying that claim. The question is whether each source obtained its evidence independently or whether the sources share a common upstream origin. If they share an origin, they do not corroborate each other — they amplify a single source.

---

## Corroborating vs. Laundering

**Corroborating sources** reached the same conclusion through independent paths:
- Different reporters with different sources
- Different researchers with different datasets
- Different organizations with different methodologies
- Physical evidence that cannot have been coordinated

**Laundering sources** reached the same conclusion through a shared path:
- One outlet reported it; others picked it up citing the first
- Multiple studies used the same underlying dataset
- Multiple experts all citing the same original paper
- Wire service story republished with different bylines

Citation laundering can be intentional or unintentional. Often it is neither — it is simply how information spreads. The effect on epistemic confidence is the same: apparent corroboration that is actually single-source.

---

## Tracing to Origin

For any claim with multiple sources:

1. Pick the earliest report of the claim (often not the one you found first)
2. Identify what primary evidence that report cites
3. For each subsequent source, check whether they cite the same primary evidence or conducted independent verification
4. Map how many genuinely independent sources exist

In practice, many claims that appear to have extensive corroboration reduce to one or two independent sources and a cascade of republications. This is not fraud — it is how media and research ecosystems work. The epistemic status should reflect the actual independence count.

---

## Minimum Corroboration Standard

For different claim types, reasonable minimum thresholds for independent corroboration:

**Factual claims about public data:** one primary source (the data itself). Corroboration is checking the interpretation, not re-verifying the data.

**Claims about events:** two independent reporters with different access paths, or one reporter plus physical/documentary evidence.

**Claims about causation:** independent replication with different methodology, or a pre-registered study, or meta-analysis of multiple independent studies.

**Claims about intent or motivation:** direct evidence (statements, documents) plus behavioral evidence. Behavioral evidence alone is not sufficient — many behaviors are consistent with multiple motivations.

---

## When Corroboration Is Insufficient

Corroboration establishes that multiple parties reached the same conclusion. It does not establish that the conclusion is correct when:

- All sources share a structural incentive to reach that conclusion (industry-funded research, government-aligned media)
- The corroborating sources could not realistically have independent access (classified information, closed proceedings)
- The claim is about something for which corroboration is structurally impossible (unique events, individual internal states)

In these cases, corroboration raises confidence but cannot establish verification. Document the limit explicitly.

---

## Detecting Citation Laundering

Signs that apparent corroboration is laundering:

- All sources cite the same original report in their chain
- Reports appeared in rapid succession after a single initial story
- The phrasing of the claim is similar or identical across sources
- No source claims to have independent access to the underlying evidence
- When you trace the chain, all paths lead to the same document or person

If you detect laundering, revert to the epistemic status of the single original source. The claim has one source, not many.
