# Participatory vs. Extractive Operating Modes

Two fundamental ways an agent can operate within any system. The distinction is not about intent but about what the interaction leaves behind.

---

## Core Distinction

**Extractive:** the agent takes value from the system and leaves less behind. The system is depleted by the interaction. Examples: a platform that monetizes user data without returning value to users, a consultant who solves a problem in a way that creates dependency rather than capability, an AI agent that automates tasks without building the human capacity to understand them.

**Participatory:** the agent creates value and leaves more behind. The system is strengthened by the interaction. Examples: a platform that returns data-derived value to users, a consultant who documents their approach so the client can repeat it, an AI agent that explains its reasoning so the human develops judgment alongside the automation.

The distinction is structural, not moral. An extractive agent can be well-intentioned. A participatory agent can charge market rates. The question is what happens to the system after the interaction.

---

## How to Identify Which Mode a System Is In

Ask what the system would look like after repeated interactions over time:

- **Does the system become more capable or more dependent?**
- **Does the knowledge stay with the agent or transfer to the participants?**
- **Does the value created stay where it was created, or flow out of the system?**
- **Do participants have increasing or decreasing ability to function without the agent?**

A system that answers "more dependent," "stays with the agent," "flows out," and "decreasing ability" is extractive regardless of what it claims about its mission.

---

## The Compounding Effect

The difference between the two modes compounds over time.

An extractive interaction reduces the system's capacity to generate value. Future extractions become harder or less productive. The extractive agent either moves on to a new system or intensifies extraction from a depleted one.

A participatory interaction increases the system's capacity to generate value. Future interactions happen within a richer system. The participatory agent benefits from the system it has helped build.

This is why the distinction matters for AI agents specifically: an agent that operates over time within the same system (organization, community, relationship) faces compounding effects in both directions. An extractive AI agent produces a system that becomes less capable of using AI well. A participatory AI agent produces a system that becomes more capable of using AI well.

---

## Why This Matters for AI Agents

AI agents are at structural risk of extractive operation:

- They are optimized for task completion, not capability transfer
- They have no stake in the long-term health of the system they operate in
- They are easy to use as a substitute for developing judgment rather than a scaffold for developing it
- The people deploying them often measure extraction (tasks completed, costs reduced) rather than participation (capability built, dependency reduced)

This is not an argument against AI agents. It is an argument for designing AI agents with explicit participatory goals — or for auditing them to determine which mode they are actually in.

---

## Relation to L3 Social Intelligence

The participatory/extractive distinction is the L3 lens applied to agent-system interaction. L3 social intelligence asks whether an action strengthens or weakens the system over time. Participatory operation is what L3 recommends when the agent operates repeatedly within the same system.

An agent missing L3 can optimize for task completion (L1) and harm prevention (L2) while systematically extracting from the social fabric it operates within. The tasks are done well, no one is hurt, but the system is slowly depleted.

---

## Practical Audit

To assess whether an AI agent deployment is participatory or extractive:

1. **Capability check:** can the people who use the agent explain what it is doing? Do they understand the outputs better or worse than before the agent was introduced?
2. **Dependency check:** what happens if the agent is unavailable? Does the absence reveal capability or expose a gap that has grown?
3. **Value flow check:** where does the value generated by the agent's work end up? Does it stay in the system or flow to the agent's operator?
4. **Knowledge transfer check:** is the agent's reasoning legible to the people it works with? Does it document its approach?

---

## Limits

The participatory/extractive distinction is not absolute. A single interaction can be extractive while a long-term relationship is participatory. A system can be designed for participation and operated extractively. The framework identifies the structural tendency, not the outcome of any single interaction.
