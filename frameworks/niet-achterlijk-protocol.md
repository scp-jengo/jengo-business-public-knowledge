# Niet-Achterlijk Protocol — Epistemological Verification Framework

A structured method for evaluating claims, sources, and conclusions without being naive or dismissive. The name translates roughly as "not-stupid protocol" — the goal is rigorous evaluation that neither accepts claims uncritically nor rejects them reflexively.

---

## Core Principle

**The question is never "is this source trustworthy?" but "what does this specific claim require to be true, and is that requirement met?"**

A trustworthy source can make a false claim. An untrustworthy source can be accidentally correct. Evaluate the claim, not the messenger.

---

## The Four Tiers

### Tier 1 — Directly Verifiable
The claim can be checked against primary sources you can access yourself.
- Public records, official statistics, court documents
- Peer-reviewed papers (check methodology, not just abstract)
- Video/audio of the event itself
- Government publications, regulatory filings

*Standard: If you can check it, check it.*

### Tier 2 — Indirectly Verifiable
The claim is consistent with Tier 1 sources and requires an inference step.
- News reports from multiple independent outlets
- Expert consensus with documented methodology
- Claims that follow logically from verified Tier 1 data

*Standard: Check the inference, not just the conclusion.*

### Tier 3 — Plausible but Unverified
The claim is internally consistent and not contradicted by Tier 1/2 sources, but cannot be directly verified.
- Witness accounts without corroboration
- Expert opinion without published methodology
- Historical claims without surviving documentation

*Standard: Label explicitly as Tier 3. Do not present as fact.*

### Tier 4 — Contradicted or Unfalsifiable
The claim contradicts Tier 1/2 evidence, or is structured so it cannot be tested.
- Conspiracy claims where all contradicting evidence is "part of the conspiracy"
- Unfalsifiable predictions ("things will get worse")
- Claims from sources with documented fabrication history

*Standard: Reject until Tier 1/2 evidence emerges.*

---

## Five Verification Questions

For any significant claim, ask in order:

1. **What would this claim require to be true?** (Identify the necessary conditions)
2. **Is there Tier 1 evidence for those conditions?** (Check primary sources)
3. **Who benefits if this claim is believed?** (Identify the interest structure)
4. **Has the source been correct before on similar claims?** (Track record, not trust)
5. **What evidence would falsify this claim?** (If none exists, flag as Tier 4)

---

## The Comfort Warning

> Increased comfort with a source's framing = increased alertness, not decreased scrutiny.

When a source consistently aligns with your worldview, the risk of uncritical acceptance increases. Apply the protocol more rigorously, not less, to sources you find compelling.

---

## Hypocrisy Test

Before accepting a diagnosis from any source, ask: **does the source apply the same standard to itself?**

A source that correctly identifies corporate capture in others but accepts funding from similar entities has a structural conflict. The diagnosis may still be correct — evaluate the claim, not only the source — but document the conflict explicitly.

---

## Causal Direction Check

Many true correlations are presented with the wrong causal arrow.

- "Isolated men radicalize online" — does isolation cause radicalization, or does pre-existing ideology cause both isolation and online behavior?
- Always state the causal direction explicitly and check whether the evidence supports that direction specifically.

---

## Application

This protocol applies to:
- News and media evaluation
- Research and academic claims
- Expert opinion and consensus statements
- Historical and archival claims
- AI-generated content (treat as Tier 3 by default until verified)
