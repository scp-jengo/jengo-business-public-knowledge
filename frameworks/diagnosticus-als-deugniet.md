# The Diagnostician-as-Problem Pattern

A structural pattern where someone who correctly identifies a problem becomes part of the problem through their response to it.

---

## Definition

The pattern has three components occurring together:

1. **Correct diagnosis** — the person accurately identifies a real dysfunction, failure, or harmful pattern
2. **Inside position** — the person gains access, authority, or benefit from the diagnosed system
3. **Unacknowledged agenda** — the diagnostic role serves interests that are not disclosed or examined

When all three are present, the diagnosis — however accurate — functions to reproduce the system rather than correct it.

---

## Why This Matters

A correct diagnosis is not sufficient evidence that the diagnostician is working against the problem. The mechanism is subtle because:

- The diagnosis can be 100% accurate
- The diagnostician may be sincere
- The solution offered may be real

The structural question is: **who benefits, and does the benefit depend on the problem continuing?**

---

## The Carlson Structure (60/40 Pattern)

A sophisticated variant: a source is ~60% accurate on diagnosis and uses that accuracy to immunize the remaining ~40%, which serves undisclosed interests or contains significant errors.

The accurate portion builds trust. The inaccurate portion leverages that trust. The ratio is what makes the pattern effective — a purely false source is dismissed; a 60% accurate source is defended by its audience.

*Detection*: identify the claims that would cost the source something if true. Those are the ones to verify most carefully.

---

## Common Instances

**The Corporate Capture Pattern**
Environmental organization correctly diagnoses corporate environmental damage → accepts funding from the diagnosed corporation → becomes advocate for the corporation's preferred regulatory approach.

**The Platform Reformer**
Journalist or researcher correctly identifies platform harms → gains platform access and monetization → critique softens as dependency deepens.

**The Internal Critic**
Employee or insider correctly identifies organizational dysfunction → uses diagnosis to secure protected status → dysfunction continues because the diagnosis serves as substitute for change.

---

## The Self-Application Test

This pattern applies universally — including to whoever is applying this framework.

Ask of any diagnostic work, including your own:
- Does my income or access depend on the problem I'm diagnosing?
- Would I apply the same standard to organizations I support?
- Am I offering diagnosis as a service, or as a pathway to correction?

---

## Distinction: Diagnosis vs. Extraction

**Diagnosis that leads to correction**: the diagnostician's role diminishes as the problem is solved. Success is measured by the problem's disappearance.

**Diagnosis as extraction**: the diagnostician's role grows as the problem persists. Success is measured by audience, revenue, or access — all of which depend on the problem remaining visible.

The question is not motivation (both can be sincere) but structure: does the diagnostic role functionally require the problem to continue?

---

## Related Patterns

- **Chosen-one validation content**: diagnosis of audience's suffering + offer of special status + enrollment in diagnostician's system
- **Institutional capture**: external critic absorbed by the institution → critique becomes legitimization
- **Vruchttest (fruit test)**: evaluate systems by what they actually produce, not what they claim to pursue
